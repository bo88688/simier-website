The Workshop

Not Just a Factory.
A Place Where Things Are Made Right.

Walk into our Foshan workshop on any given Tuesday, and you'll see the same thing: people who genuinely care about the piece in front of them. A cutter measuring twice before touching blade to fabric. A finisher running their hand along a seam, checking for a smoothness only fingertips can detect. A QC inspector turning a chair frame over, looking — really looking — at every joint.

This isn't a show. It's just how we work.

We don't believe in "good enough." Every SIMIER product passes through 27 distinct quality checkpoints before it earns the right to ship. That sounds like a lot. It is. But when your furniture is going to live in someone's home — in the light of a California morning or the quiet of a Paris evening — it deserves that kind of attention.

This page is a tour of where it all happens. You'll see the floor, meet some of the team, and get a sense of how a sketch on a designer's desk becomes the sofa in your living room.

From Raw Material to Your Home

The Journey of a Piece

Every sofa, chair, and table follows a deliberate path through our workshop. Here's what that looks like.

01

The Frame Comes First

Solid kiln-dried ash, oak, or walnut — depending on the design — cut and joined by hand. We use traditional mortise-and-tenon joinery reinforced with corner blocks. No shortcuts. A SIMIER sofa frame carries a 2-year structural warranty because we know exactly how it was built.

WoodSolid Ash / Oak / Walnut
JoineryMortise & Tenon + Corner Blocks
Warranty2 Years on Frame
02

Layered for Comfort

Comfort isn't one density. It's a recipe. We layer high-resilience foam (38–42 kg/m³), feather-down toppers, and Dacron wrapping to create a seat that feels soft when you sit but holds its shape for years. Every cushion is cut, wrapped, and tested on our floor.

CoreHigh-Resilience Foam 38–42 kg/m³
TopperFeather-Down Blend
WrapDacron Fiber
03

The Fabric, Cut and Sewn

Our Italian-imported tech fabrics, velvets, and microfiber arrive on rolls. Each hide or bolt is inspected under daylight lamps for color consistency and flaws. Patterns are laid by hand — never auto-nested by software — because a human eye catches things an algorithm misses. Seams are double-stitched at stress points. Every cover is cut to be removable and machine-washable.

FabricsItalian Tech-Linen / Velvet / Microfiber
InspectionDaylight-Lamp Color Check
FeatureRemovable & Machine-Washable Covers
04

Assembled. Inspected. Signed Off.

Frame meets foam meets fabric. Each piece is assembled on a dedicated station by a single craftsperson who sees it through from start to finish. They sign a QC card that stays with the piece. Then it moves to our independent inspection team — a separate group whose only job is to find problems before you do. Their rejection rate averages 12%. We're proud of that number.

AssemblySingle-Craftsperson Per Piece
InspectionIndependent QC Team, 27 Checkpoints
Sign-offQC Card on Every Order
05

Packed to Arrive Perfect

We've learned through thousands of shipments what it takes for furniture to survive a container journey intact. Double-wall corrugated cartons. Reinforced corner protectors. Moisture-barrier wrapping for ocean freight. Each package is labeled with handling instructions in the destination language. Our logistics team tracks every container from Foshan port to final delivery.

PackagingDouble-Wall Cartons + Corner Protectors
ShippingFCL / LCL from Foshan Port
TrackingEnd-to-End Container Visibility

Material Integrity

We Source With Intent. We Build With Care.

Every material that enters our workshop is documented, traceable, and inspected. Our wood comes from FSC-certified sources. Our fabrics are imported from Italian mills with decades of textile heritage. Our foams are CertiPUR-certified — no heavy metals, no ozone depleters, no formaldehyde.

We're not claiming to be the only workshop that cares about materials. But we are one of the few in our category that will open our doors and show you exactly what we use and how we use it.

Because when you're buying furniture for a home 6,000 miles away, transparency isn't a nice-to-have. It's the whole point.
