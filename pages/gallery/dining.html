The Dining Collection

Where the day
comes together.

A dining table is never just a table. It is the stage for Tuesday-night spaghetti and Saturday-night dinner parties. It holds homework and laptops, birthday cakes and condolence cards. It sees your family at their best, their worst, and their most unguarded.

Why It Matters

The table is the hardest-working
piece of furniture you own.

Think about everything your dining table does in a single week. Breakfast for one. Lunch over email. Afternoon homework. Evening dinner for four. Weekend brunch that stretches into dinner. It holds hot plates and cold drinks, elbows and laptops, candlelight and chaos. Most furniture gets to do one thing well. A dining table has to do everything.

Our tables are cut from solid hardwood slabs — American oak, black walnut, and ash — each board selected for grain character and structural stability. The finish is a hand-applied matte lacquer that protects against water rings and heat marks without looking like plastic. Run your hand across the surface. That warmth is real wood. It always will be.

And the chairs. A dining chair has to support a person through a three-hour meal without making them shift in their seat. Our chair frames use the same mortise-and-tenon joinery found in heirloom furniture. Seat depths and back angles are calibrated to actual human bodies — not industry averages from a 1970s textbook.

Solid wood. Real joinery. No shortcuts.

Every tabletop is a single slab or a matched set of boards, bookmatched for symmetry and joined with invisible glue lines. The legs are attached with traditional mortise-and-tenon — a joint that has held furniture together for three thousand years and will hold yours for at least a few decades more.

We finish with a hand-rubbed matte lacquer that seals against moisture without creating that plastic-looking gloss you see on mass-produced furniture. The wood continues to breathe. It responds to humidity. It ages. That is not a flaw — it is the whole point.
